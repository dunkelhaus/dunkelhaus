Designing rewards for a small learning agent

Rewards are the specification an agent can actually read. You can write a mission statement, a scoring rubric, or a product goal, but a reinforcement-learning agent only responds to the number it receives after each step. Design those numbers carelessly and the agent does exactly what you did not intend, in a way that looked perfectly reasonable from inside its own scorecard. This article takes a small navigation agent with tabular Q-learning and shows how each reward setting turns into behavior — and lets you reshape its incentives while it learns, inside the page.

A fixed, readable world

The environment is an 8 × 6 grid — 48 cells, walls, hazards, goal — solved entirely in memory. The agent, a cyan circle labeled A, starts at S in the lower left. The goal, a magenta diamond labeled G, sits in the upper right. Light gray crossed squares block movement; magenta triangles impose a hazard penalty on the step that enters them. Four actions move the agent up, right, down, or left; an off-grid move or a wall leaves its position unchanged.

The reward terms are small and legible: a per-step reward, a hazard penalty added to that step's reward, and a goal reward that ends an episode immediately. Any episode that has not reached the goal after 96 steps stops as a timeout. Defaults: goal $+20$, hazard $-8$, step $-0.2$.

The Q-learning update

After every transition the agent nudges one number in its table — the value it holds for taking action $a$ in state $s$:

Q(s, a) \leftarrow Q(s, a) + \alpha \left[ r \; + \; \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s', a') \; - \; Q(s, a) \right]

With the playground settings, the learning rate $\alpha = 0.25$ keeps each correction partial, and the discount $\gamma = 0.95$ lets value flow backward from the goal, one cell at a time. When the goal (or the step cap) ends an episode there is no $s'$ to look ahead to, so the terminal update drops the future-value term entirely. The cited texts develop these dynamics in depth — Q-learning converges under mild conditions.

Exploration is $\varepsilon$-greedy: the agent picks a random action with probability $\varepsilon$, decaying from $0.5$ toward $0.08$ as episodes accumulate, and otherwise takes its best-valued action.Hyperparameters: learning rate $\alpha = 0.25$, discount $\gamma = 0.95$, exploration $\varepsilon = 0.5$ decaying toward $0.08$. Ties in equal action values break by action order. Watch policy and Step exploit that best-valued action without updating Q at all, so watching an untrained agent can simply loop until the timeout — the report is honest about that.

Incentive changes worth trying

Two runs bracket the lesson. Launch the figure, accept the defaults, and press Train agent: with steps costing $-0.2$ each, the agent learns a direct, fairly safe route, since dawdling bleeds reward and hazards are clearly unhealthy. Then change the step reward to $+1$ and retrain. Now the agent earns simply for moving, so it can do better by never finishing: expect wandering loops that burn the step-cap instead of chasing the goal. The task asks for reaches-goal behavior; the reward paid for something else, and the agent obeyed the reward.

A third exposure: set the hazard penalty to $0$ and watch the policy. With no penalty for cutting through triangles, some policies route straight through hazards and still reach the goal — legal in the spec, suspicious in the behavior. That gap between "reached the goal" and "behaved the way you wanted" is the entire argument of this article.

Execution and accessibility

The playground keeps its computation in a Web Worker, so seconds of training never block the page's scrolling or reading. The figure downloads and starts only on your explicit launch — the article's initial payload excludes it — and closing the figure returns it to a relaunch state. Every control is a semantic button or input: full keyboard access, touch targets tuned for the site standard, textual progress (position, outcome, per-run metrics) alongside the canvas, and symbol labels that do not rely on color alone. Under reduced motion there is no decorative animation; Pause and Step let you advance the policy at your own tempo. Session identifiers prevent stale messages after a reset, and if the worker fails, the article stays readable with a retry button — the failure never eats the text.

Limits and honest reporting

Scale first: this is tabular Q-learning in a 48-cell world — an educational instrument, not a production RL system, and no evidence about large networks. The numbers are still real Q-learning, executed in genuine training episodes with reproducible seeds, verified by unit tests over transitions, bounds, and value updates, plus browser tests over the full launch/train/watch/reset flow. Metrics never pretend to be progress: episode counts and training returns come from executed training episodes, while policy steps and policy return come from explicit evaluation. Even at small scale the lesson generalizes: every reward function is a specification, and the behavior you get is the one you paid for.
